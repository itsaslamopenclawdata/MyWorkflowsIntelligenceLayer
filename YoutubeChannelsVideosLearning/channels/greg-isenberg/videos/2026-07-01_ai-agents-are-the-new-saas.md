# AI Agents are the new SaaS

**Channel:** Greg Isenberg  **Published:** 2026-07-01  **Duration:** 26:03  **Watch:** https://youtube.com/watch?v=83fWzQSWB10

## TL;DR
Greg argues AI agents are the new SaaS — selling work instead of tools, and the TAM is human-capital-sized, not seat-license-sized. Walks through an 8-step agent-first business playbook (product-is-the-job → pick-a-workflow-with-a-paycheck → shadow-the-human → spec-the-agent → build-minimum-useful-agent → product-wrapper-is-the-SaaS → sell-the-pilot-then-productize → distribution) and closes with a 30-day zero-to-100 launch plan. The key reframe: SaaS sold software, agents sell labor — and pricing moves from seats to outcomes.

## Key Insights
- **The mental model shift is "SaaS sells software, agent SaaS sells work."** A restaurant doesn't want a tool, it wants the phone answered and the booking made. That's the framing that turns an automation into a product (02:00).
- **Pick a workflow with a paycheck attached.** If people already pay an employee/agency/receptionist for the work, there's a wedge. Score jobs on 5 axes: frequency, pain cost, clear finish line, software it touches, and who owns the budget (05:00).
- **A good agent workflow has 5 traits:** happens often (hourly > daily), has a clear finish line, touches existing software the customer uses, has edge cases that are "annoying but learnable" (not pure judgment, not pure zaps), and the buyer can feel the loss (missed calls, slow replies, empty calendar slots) (04:50).
- **Shadow the human before building.** Watch them do 10-20 real jobs, screen-record, ask "what makes a case easy? what makes it weird? what did you check before deciding? where do mistakes happen?" The detail is the product (07:30).
- **The agent spec needs 7 parts:** trigger (what wakes it), context (what it knows), tools (what it can use), autonomy boundary (what it can do itself), approval gates, escalation rules, and a success metric (08:45).
- **The "Minimum Useful Agent" ladder:** draft-and-approve → triage → coordinator → bounded-action. Don't ship "fully autonomous employee" on day one — that's how you get the Twitter demos that don't work as businesses (10:00).
- **Anthropic's point that "many agent problems should start as workflows":** workflows follow predictable paths, agents decide dynamically. Earn autonomy by starting with a predictable path and adding judgment only where it creates value (11:40).
- **The product wrapper is what makes it SaaS.** Agents do the work; the wrapper creates trust — logs, approvals, controls, handoff rules, a way to test before going live. "The dashboard can be really simple. But the customer still needs the control room" (12:50).
- **Evals are both QA and sales asset.** Run the agent against 50 real examples, mark the right answers, then publish the score to the buyer: "We tested this on 50 of your old maintenance requests. It routed 42 correctly, flagged 6 for human review, made 2 mistakes. Here are the 2 mistakes and how we fix them." (14:00).
- **Sell the pilot like labor, then productize.** Start with 3 customers, 1 niche, 1 workflow, 1 pain. Charge a setup fee + simple monthly fee ($1,500 setup + $1,000/mo for one workflow, or $2,000 setup + $30 per qualified appointment, or $3,000/mo up to 500 handled tickets). Then productize what repeats (15:50).
- **Workflow teardowns are the distribution unlock.** Show the old way (call comes in, nobody answers, CSR asks 5 questions, forgets to follow up, owner's heart breaks) vs the agent way (agent answers, asks right questions, checks service area, books, updates CRM, flags edge cases). "Painkillers, not vitamins" (18:45).
- **The 30-day plan:** Day 1 pick a niche where missed work costs money. Day 2 interview 10 operators, screen-share the workflow, pay them. Day 3 pick one workflow with frequency/pain/software/success metric. Day 4 write the agent spec. Day 5 run it manually with AI + human approval. Day 6 build the smallest useful version. Day 7 create the eval set from 50 real examples. Week 2 sell 2 pilots in the same niche. Week 3 add the product wrapper. Week 4 publish teardowns, turn pilots into proof, double down on content (22:00).
- **Why agents > SaaS in TAM:** software moved from "help me do the work" to "do the work with me" — and the addressable market is human capital, which is multi-trillion. Most people see the shift, almost nobody is building agent-first businesses (24:30).
